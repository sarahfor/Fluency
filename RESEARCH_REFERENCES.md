# Fluent research references

Reviewed November 8, 2026. These references use author–date citations. The note after each citation states its role in Fluent. A cited source is not evidence that Fluent itself improves learning; that question requires evaluation with Fluent learners.

## AI literacy and interaction design

- Long, D., & Magerko, B. (2020). What is AI literacy? Competencies and design considerations. In *Proceedings of the 2020 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems* (pp. 1–16). ACM. [https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376727](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376727) — A framework of AI-literacy competencies and design considerations. It informs the learning objectives; it is not an evaluation of Fluent.
- Amershi, S., Weld, D., Vorvoreanu, M., Fourney, A., Nushi, B., Collisson, P., Suh, J., Iqbal, S., Bennett, P. N., Inkpen, K., Teevan, J., Kikin-Gil, R., & Horvitz, E. (2019). Guidelines for human–AI interaction. In *Proceedings of the 2019 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems* (pp. 1–13). ACM. [https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233) — Design guidance for expectations, feedback, correction, and user control. It informs interaction choices, not learning-outcome claims.

## Retrieval practice and language-learning research

- Roediger, H. L., III, & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. *Psychological Science, 17*(3), 249–255. [https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x) — Evidence for retrieval practice with studied prose passages; motivates opportunities to recall concepts after introduction.
- Smith, M. A., & Karpicke, J. D. (2014). Retrieval practice with short-answer, multiple-choice, and hybrid tests. *Memory, 22*(7), 784–802. [https://doi.org/10.1080/09658211.2013.831454](https://doi.org/10.1080/09658211.2013.831454) — Compares retrieval question formats and the role of successful retrieval; informs varied answer formats.
- Settles, B., & Meeder, B. (2016). A trainable spaced repetition model for language learning. In *Proceedings of the 54th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics* (Vol. 1, pp. 1848–1858). Association for Computational Linguistics. [https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/P16-1174](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/P16-1174) — Describes an adaptive review model trained on Duolingo data. **Fluent does not currently implement this model or adaptive scheduling.**
- Jiang, X., Rollinson, J., Plonsky, L., Gustafson, E., & Pajak, B. (2021). Evaluating the reading and listening outcomes of beginning-level Duolingo courses. *Foreign Language Annals, 54*(4), 974–1002. [https://doi.org/10.1111/flan.12600](https://doi.org/10.1111/flan.12600) — Assesses selected Spanish and French course completers' reading and listening outcomes. The journal issue is Winter 2021; the publisher records first online publication on January 11, 2022. The result does not establish Fluent's effectiveness.
- Loewen, S., Crowther, D., Isbell, D. R., Kim, K. M., Maloney, J., Miller, Z. F., & Rawal, H. (2019). Mobile-assisted language learning: A Duolingo case study. *ReCALL, 31*(3), 293–311. [https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344019000065](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344019000065) — A small language-learning case study, useful context for engagement and user experience; not evidence for AI-literacy outcomes.

## Duolingo product descriptions

These are first-party descriptions of Duolingo's product, not peer-reviewed tests of Fluent.

- Duolingo. (n.d.). *What is the Duolingo teaching method?* [https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-teaching-method/](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-teaching-method/) — Describes learning by doing, small steps, hints, and explanations.
- Duolingo. (n.d.). *How to use Duolingo for language learning.* [https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-101-how-to-learn-a-language-on-duolingo/](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-101-how-to-learn-a-language-on-duolingo/) — Describes lessons, XP, streaks, and other game features.
- Duolingo. (n.d.). *Understanding Duolingo's Time Spent Learning Well metric.* [https://blog.duolingo.com/time-spent-learning-well/](https://blog.duolingo.com/time-spent-learning-well/) — Explains Duolingo's own practice-oriented product metric; Fluent does not use or claim this metric.
- Duolingo. (n.d.). *Here are 7 ways to practice with Duolingo right now.* [https://blog.duolingo.com/ways-to-practice-in-duolingo/](https://blog.duolingo.com/ways-to-practice-in-duolingo/) — Gives examples of review, matching, timed challenges, and other exercise formats.

## Word embeddings and bias

- Bolukbasi, T., Chang, K.-W., Zou, J. Y., Saligrama, V., & Kalai, A. T. (2016). Man is to computer programmer as woman is to homemaker? Debiasing word embeddings. In *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems* (Vol. 29). [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/a486cd07e4ac3d270571622f4f316ec5-Abstract.html) · [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06520) — Studies gender stereotypes in **static word embeddings**. It informs the historical example in The Word Map, which uses authored toy data rather than the paper's trained vectors.
- Gonen, H., & Goldberg, Y. (2019). Lipstick on a pig: Debiasing methods cover up systematic gender biases in word embeddings but do not remove them. In *Proceedings of the 2019 Conference of the North American Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics: Human Language Technologies* (Vol. 1, pp. 609–614). Association for Computational Linguistics. [https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/N19-1061](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/N19-1061) — Tests limits of **two** static-embedding debiasing methods. Useful further reading; it does not show that all bias-mitigation methods fail.
- Harvard Business School. (2024). *AI & inequality: Harvard psychologist Mahzarin Banaji on AI, implicit bias, and social ethics* [Video]. [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SxtSkKxxO3Q) — A public discussion for optional context, not a peer-reviewed research paper.

## Related research for future design questions

These papers were supplied as additional reading. They are **not** evidence for the current games or claims that Fluent improves learning.

- Sun, Y., Liao, M., Sundar, S. S., & Walther, J. B. (2026). Does transparency matter when an AI system meets performance expectations? An experiment with an online dating site. *Computers in Human Behavior, 177*, Article 108875. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108875](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108875) — Context-specific experiment on AI transparency and user responses; a possible prompt for later transparency-design research.
- Yu, A., Li, C., Macesanu, L., Balaji, A., Ray, R., Mooney, R., & Martín-Martín, R. (2026). Mixed-initiative dialog for human-robot collaborative manipulation. In *2026 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*. [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05535) · [project page](https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/MicoBot/) — Studies human-robot collaboration; relevant only if Fluent later teaches mixed-initiative interaction. The arXiv DOI identifies the manuscript, not the conference proceedings.
